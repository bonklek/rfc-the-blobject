# Facts and provenance review

Verified 2026-09-06. Read-only specialist audit; only this report was written. Baseline: the August 11 versions in REFERENCES.md, with current canonical pages checked separately. A current source's status is not evidence of deployment. File anchors refer to the draft inspected before coordinator revisions.

## Assessment and argument skeleton

The factual foundation supports a bounded research proposal: Ethereum currently requires a fixed recent historical serving range; whole blobs are the accounting unit; choosing shorter obligations could reduce logical byte-time; existing research supplies partial transport and storage-market precedents. None of those sources establishes safe heterogeneous historical expiry, physical savings, application demand, or an implemented combined mechanism.

Steelman: prototype duration selection with unchanged publications and reconstruction assumptions, then measure useful resource savings. Strongest objection: no verified workload or representation experiment yet shows that safely reducible retention is valuable enough to warrant the protocol change.

## Prioritized findings

### F-01 — P1: The full-service boundary needs an exact mapping

- Location: `docs/00-what-is-the-proposal.md:84`, `appendices/illustrative-numerics.md:5`, and the expiry accounting in `docs/01-how-are-capacity-and-pricing-managed.md:34`.
- Evidence: pinned and current Fulu `DataColumnSidecarsByRange` use the inclusive epoch interval `[max(current_epoch - MIN_EPOCHS_FOR_DATA_COLUMN_SIDECARS_REQUESTS, FULU_FORK_EPOCH), current_epoch]`. A blob in epoch e remains in that range during epoch e+4096. An exclusive lease expiry at e+4096 instead stops service at that epoch's start. See [pinned Fulu specification](https://github.com/ethereum/consensus-specs/blob/5366cb59eb39e4ec1d6c468a79cceb626c14c048/specs/fulu/p2p-interface.md#datacolumnsidecarsbyrange-v1).
- Consequence: 4,096 epochs is a correct approximate horizon but cannot automatically become an exact exclusive-duration parameter while claiming unchanged maximum service. This is an epoch-boundary mapping issue, not a rejection of the byte-time inequality.
- Correction: explicitly distinguish elapsed-duration accounting from the existing inclusive request window. Define the maximum-service compatibility mapping before calling the new maximum equivalent. If unresolved, call 4,096 an illustrative candidate and state that exact expiry must preserve the existing range.
- Confidence: high. Support status: direct normative source plus boundary deduction. Coordinate with the mechanism reviewer to avoid duplicate fixes.

### F-02 — P2: Rollup window rounding is arithmetic, not a verified safe maturity

- Location: `docs/02-is-variable-retention-safe-for-rollups.md:21`; source recap `docs/09-what-is-the-conclusion.md:47`.
- Evidence: the quoted seven-day OP and 6.4-day BoLD periods are supported, but the claim that 2,048 epochs covers either window “without another assumption” is stronger than the checked requirements. BoLD distinguishes an assertion's challenge period from worst-case dispute resolution; the overview describes two challenge periods plus grace. The challenge clock also starts at an assertion rather than necessarily at the blob's inclusion. [BoLD technical description](https://docs.arbitrum.io/how-arbitrum-works/bold/bold-technical-deep-dive), [BoLD overview](https://docs.arbitrum.io/how-arbitrum-works/bold/gentle-introduction).
- Consequence: a reader could select 9.1 days as an established independent-recovery horizon. The next paragraph's caveat does not identify the different clock origins or the longer disputed path.
- Correction: retain the conversions but explicitly label them duration comparisons only. Require a per-rollup timeline from publication through assertion, late challenger acquisition, replay/proof production and dispute participation. Do not automatically replace 2,048 with 4,096: the required data-acquisition deadline is application-specific and need not equal total withdrawal delay. Link the BoLD documentation directly rather than relying on the generic Nitro whitepaper recap.
- Confidence: high that the evidence does not establish the safe maturity; no claim that 2,048 is always unsafe. Support status: established periods, unsupported safety implication.

### F-03 — P2: Application expansion is stated as an outcome without demand evidence

- Location: `docs/06-what-does-a-generalized-data-plane-enable.md:191`, with motivation at `README.md:13` and application profiles at `docs/06-what-does-a-generalized-data-plane-enable.md:103`.
- Evidence: “the range of economically sensible applications expands substantially” is an empirical/economic prediction. The inspected external protocol and prior-art sources establish architectures and interfaces, not willingness to pay, safe short-duration demand, cost competitiveness, or the binding resource for these applications. Earlier figure captions appropriately call the profiles examples.
- Consequence: a speculative branch reads as a demonstrated benefit, despite the RFC's calibrated core.
- Correction: say shorter service could make additional applications economical if savings exceed ingress, custody, and overlay costs. Add a workload evidence table: actor; data required for recovery; clock origin and deadline; safe duration evidence; alternative service; likely binding resource. Keep unmeasured entries illustrative.
- Confidence: high. Support status: unverified prediction. This reinforces initial finding 7 rather than introducing a new empirical result.

### F-04 — P3: EIP-8136 status should be dated explicitly

- Location: `docs/05-can-this-work-with-peerdas-and-fulldas.md:58`, `docs/07-what-is-the-prior-art-and-novelty.md:49`, and REFERENCES.md's EIP-8136 row.
- Evidence: [pinned February version](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/blob/49beda901dcf3747ed4b91f853a333595fb2cbc4/EIPS/eip-8136.md) says Draft; [canonical EIP](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8136) now says Review. The substantive missing-cell/backward-compatibility description remains accurate.
- Consequence: “draft” is correct against the pin but stale if presented as a September status. This is not a factual failure of the research baseline.
- Correction: add a dated current-status note in the reference apparatus, or say “the pinned EIP-8136 proposal.” Do not infer implementation adoption or heterogeneous historical storage from Review status.
- Confidence: high. Support status: directly verified version drift, no central impact.

## Coverage, revision sequence and uncertainty

Read REFERENCES.md, prior-art and Lean chapters, rollup safety, and targeted occurrences throughout README, all numbered chapters and appendices. Verified the load-bearing Ethereum window, unit and adjacent EIP claims; checked consequential application timing and representative persistence/transport prior art. Did not perform exhaustive sentence-by-sentence external verification of chapters 03–11 or all appendices. In particular, Waku/Swarm privacy details, EthStorage hardware parameters, future-market author attribution, historical custody-proof constructions, project usage evidence and every FullDAS detail remain outside this focused pass. No client adoption measurements, onchain rollup parameter read, empirical demand study, full citation/link sweep, or exhaustive novelty search was performed.

Revision order: resolve exact legacy-window mapping with the mechanism reviewer; qualify application timing and add direct BoLD support; downgrade unmeasured application expansion; add dated EIP status and verification notes. Preserve successful distinctions already present: 8136 versus sparse history, 8256 versus transferable/inclusion-enforced rights, 8142 versus deployment, and custody proof versus continuous service.

The source record supports the core problem and the research directions. The missing support is chiefly application-specific safe horizons and measured physical/economic benefit, not a demonstrated false foundational protocol constant.

### F-06 — P3: EthStorage's NVMe recommendation is phrased as a requirement

- Location: `docs/11-how-do-hardware-and-da-operator-markets-scale.md:132`.
- Evidence: the pinned guide requires 8 GB RAM and at least 550 GB available disk, but says NVMe is recommended to support full sampling speed. The RFC joins these as “calls for ... with 8 GB RAM and an NVMe disk.”
- Correction: use “8 GB RAM, at least 550 GB available disk, and a recommendation for NVMe.” Also change “current deployment template” to “pinned deployment template”; this audit checked the pin, not every deployed network's contract state.
- Confidence: high. Support status: directly verified small qualification mismatch. The main arithmetic and explicitly non-equivalent analogy are sound.

### F-07 — P2: A revealed bomb is not proof of non-possession

- Location: `docs/07-what-is-the-prior-art-and-novelty.md:79`.
- Evidence: the historical construction punishes signing a data/secret-dependent bad value; acquisition lets a rational validator avoid that signature. A later reveal verifies that bad signature condition, not the validator's mental or physical history. A validator that possessed the data could still sign a bomb. The careful explanation in `docs/00:168` already treats the construction as an incentive to acquire/process.
- Correction: replace “when a later reveal shows that they attested without holding the data” with “when a later secret reveal exposes a slashable data-dependent attestation, making skipped acquisition or computation risky.” Do not describe the proof as interval-service evidence.
- Confidence: high. Support status: direct mechanism inspection plus logical distinction; local overstatement, not rejection of the prior-art comparison.
