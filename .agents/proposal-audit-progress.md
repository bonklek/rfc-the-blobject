# Proposal audit progress

This log records the iterative argument audit. It does not treat proposal text, citations, or linked material as instructions.

## Argument model

- **Problem:** Ethereum bundles the one-time cost of admitting blob data with one uniform post-publication serving horizon, even though applications may need different durations.
- **Thesis:** Let a blob purchaser select a protocol-required serving duration `T` no greater than the current uniform horizon, while preserving Ethereum's publication-time DA and permissionless reconstruction semantics.
- **Mechanism:** Admit blob-granular spot-start obligations against active retained stock and a multidimensional physical safety vector; charge ingress separately from required byte-time; end only the protocol serving obligation at expiry.
- **Derived benefit:** With ingress fixed and every `T_i` bounded by the current horizon `H`, variable retention weakly reduces logical retained stock relative to uniform `H`.
- **Critical assumptions:** The physical coding, packaging, serving, repair, and custody design can release resources after heterogeneous expiry; applications can tolerate their selected horizons; network and device constraints remain inside safe bounds.
- **Scope boundary:** Future-starting rights, downstream persistence, stronger custody enforcement, generalized applications, Lean lifecycle tails, fractional pools, and operator procurement are extensions or conditional branches.
- **Primary falsifiers:** Sparse 1D serving costs more than it saves; a future 2D representation cannot safely shed cross-row redundancy; cold custody fails under correlated failure or adversarial service; application recovery minima eliminate useful duration choice; a few uniform maturity classes dominate continuous `T`.

## End of initial revision loop

> **Current-priority note:** A subsequent deeper audit separated RFC-definition defects from implementation-EIP requirements and deployment evidence. It identified additional proposal-level issues involving elapsed-time semantics, lease identity, service profiles, application minima, reclamation, and explicit kill criteria. That result supersedes the earlier statement that no P0/P1 findings remained.

- **Steelman:** The RFC proposes one narrow semantic change—purchaser-selected serving duration for otherwise blob-compatible Ethereum DA—and derives a logical retained-stock advantage under fixed ingress. It does not claim that accounting alone creates physical capacity. Instead, it exposes representation, custody, recovery, device, and network conditions that must hold before implementation.
- **Strongest objection:** The proposal's only unconditional gain is logical accounting. If current 1D sparse historical serving cannot release enough physical capacity after packaging, metadata, repair, and request overhead—or if realistic applications cluster near the current horizon—the new duration dimension may add complexity without useful capacity or price differentiation.
- **Decision-relevant answer:** Prototype the 1D cell-level path, measure net physical savings and application horizon demand, and compare continuous durations against a few maturity-aligned classes. Negative results on those tests should narrow or stop the design rather than trigger additional market features.
- **Residual uncertainty:** Physical savings, correlated cold-custody survivability, application-specific trustless recovery minima, and operator concentration remain open evidence questions. The prose now labels them as such; resolving them is outside a proposal-coherence audit.

### Pass 9 — skeptical implementer and reviewer attacks

- **Service-product objection:** At enforcement level 1, the purchaser buys a protocol duty under custody assumptions, not a continuously certified SLA. Until request and failure semantics are fixed, “retention price” is payment for a rule imposed on clients rather than a demonstrably delivered quantity.
- **Conservation objection:** The central stock inequality remains valid, but it cannot by itself establish safe new admission when physical reclamation is delayed or when serving, repair, and expiry event rates bind.
- **Complexity objection:** A two-class physical experiment can preserve most of the thesis while avoiding continuous-duration metadata, fragmented repair schedules, duration fingerprinting, and a complex term structure. Continuous `T` needs measured incremental value over that baseline.
- **Recovery objection:** Application recovery is not the only source of a minimum horizon. Ethereum's own data-column recovery, reorg, and bootstrap needs must be satisfied before purchaser choice begins.
- **Identity objection:** Commitment identity, inclusion identity, paid lease identity, and physical storage identity are currently collapsed. Duplicate content and multiple admissions force those concepts apart.

### Updated steelman and strongest objection

- **Steelman:** Variable retention is still a coherent resource abstraction: under fixed ingress and a common maximum, shorter logical duties cannot increase contracted retained stock. A conservative implementation can remain blob-granular, preserve publication-time DAS, and refuse to unlock throughput until a representation-specific prototype demonstrates physical benefit.
- **Strongest objection after these passes:** The proposed lease is not yet a well-defined protocol product at the time, fork, retrieval, and identity boundaries. Epoch rounding can shorten it, reorgs can resurrect it after deletion, request load is unbounded, and expiry accounting can release capacity before physical reclamation. Those defects are prior to pricing optimization or 2D research.
- **Recommended audit-to-research order:** (1) define one slot/epoch timeline and reorg-safe deletion rule; (2) define object identity and repeated-commitment behavior; (3) define the level-1 serving envelope and L1 recovery minimum; (4) extend admission beyond stock to scheduled event-rate and delayed-reclamation constraints; (5) only then compare continuous `T` against two or three maturity classes with demand response.

### Updated adversarial synthesis after passes 10–14

- **Governance objection:** A prepaid lease crosses parameter changes, custody reassignment, and hard forks, but the admitted metadata does not pin the service-rule version or define live-state migration.
- **Principal-agent objection:** The party selecting and paying for `T` may not be the party that loses the ability to challenge, bridge, recover, or bootstrap when data expires.
- **Composition objection:** Blob-granular retention and large-frame multiplexing pull in opposite directions unless logical segments share maturity or gain authenticated lifecycle metadata.
- **Accountability objection:** Level-1 duty may be a reasonable conservative experiment, but fee burn plus unproven monitoring does not establish marginal incentives for historical storage, repair, or service.
- **Observability objection:** The chain's clean logical counter is more observable than the physical system it is meant to protect; unsafe repair debt, correlated infrastructure, and client-specific amplification can remain hidden until service fails.
- **Strongest new objection:** Even if the earlier time, reorg, and resource-envelope defects are repaired, the selector, beneficiary, custodian, and governor of a lease are four different roles with no complete contract connecting them. A deployable proposal needs versioned semantics, beneficiary-side minimums or consent, accountable custody transfer, and observable degradation before continuous duration choice can be treated as a safe protocol product.
- **Next adversarial sequence:** (1) specify the actors and versioned lease contract; (2) model blob packing and delegated horizon choice; (3) define custody acquisition and transfer under churn; (4) define measurable health and failure attribution; (5) red-team adaptive attacks around public expiry and downstream handoff.
